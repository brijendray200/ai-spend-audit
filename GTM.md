# GTM

The exact target user is an engineering manager or founder at a 10–80 person startup that adopted multiple AI tools quickly in 2024–2026 and now has no clean owner for AI software spend. This is usually a company that has real product velocity pressure, at least a few managers buying tools ad hoc, and enough seat growth that “just expense it” stopped being harmless. The person most likely to care first is the engineering manager who gets renewal surprises or the founder who notices the AI bill rising faster than headcount.

Right before they want this tool, they are usually doing one of a few things: reviewing monthly SaaS spend, preparing for a board update, cleaning up vendor renewals, or reading social posts about “what AI stack are you paying for?” They are not searching broad phrases like “reduce costs.” They are searching or scrolling narrower intent: “Cursor vs Copilot for teams,” “ChatGPT Team worth it,” “Anthropic API pricing,” “AI tools every dev actually uses,” or founder discussions about duplicate subscriptions.

The first 100 users in 30 days should come from narrow founder and operator channels, not generic content marketing. I would post teardown-style examples on X and LinkedIn showing anonymized audit outcomes with exact savings math. I would share a free “AI spend per engineer” calculator version inside communities like r/SaaS, r/startups, Indie Hackers, SaaStr and RevGenius Slack groups, and founder/operator WhatsApp or Discord circles where people already trade tooling advice. I would also cold DM 30 founders or engineering leads with a personalized note: “I built a free tool that shows whether your AI stack is overpriced. Want me to run your current stack through it and send the report?”

The unfair distribution channel for Credex is that the company already sits adjacent to the purchase itself. Most content creators can tell you to switch tools. Credex can tell you when to keep the same vendor and simply buy better. That makes the pitch more credible because the company is not forced into “rip everything out and replace it” advice. If the product works, week-1 traction should look like 150 landing visitors, 35 completed audits, 10 captured emails, and 3 consultation-worthy accounts with more than $500 in monthly savings potential. Those are modest numbers, but they are enough to prove whether the message is resonating.
